A destination and resort guide for couples, any time of year: which regions and property styles fit the way the two of you travel.

The short answer

The best romantic getaways start with the resort style, not the postcard. Adults-only all-inclusives suit couples who want simplicity, boutique hotels and villas suit privacy, overwater bungalows are a splurge, and a city break suits couples who would rather walk than lounge. I match the region and property to how you travel, then confirm the room category and inclusions before I recommend anything.

Most of the couples I work with do not need help choosing a beach. They need help choosing the right kind of place, because romantic getaways succeed or fail on the property and the room, not on the name of the island. I plan romantic getaways year round, and my first questions are the same in February and September: how do the two of you like to spend a day, how far will you fly, and what does privacy mean to you. A couple who wants a quiet pool and a couple who wants a new restaurant nightly should not end up at the same resort.

This page is my working guide to those choices: the resort styles that suit couples and who each is for, where each style is strongest, when each destination is at its best, and how I read room categories and inclusions before a property reaches your shortlist. Romantic getaways planned this way feel effortless because the fit was right from the start. Resort styles for couples, and who each one suits

Before we talk about where, I want to know what kind of stay you are picturing, because each style trades something for something else, and the wrong one feels wrong in any setting.

Adults-only all-inclusive

One price for meals, drinks, and most activities, with no children on property. The simplest way to unplug; Punta Cana, Antigua, and Sandals are where I place many of them.

Boutique hotel

Fewer rooms, staff who learn your names, often stronger food. For couples who prefer one excellent restaurant to six pools.

Overwater bungalow

Found mainly in the Maldives, French Polynesia, and a few Caribbean spots. Worth it when the room itself is the destination.

Private villa

Your own pool, kitchen, and silence. A strong fit for longer stays and couples who want to set their own schedule, often with a private chef.

City break

Museums, restaurants, and long walks instead of a beach. Europe suits a week; a US city suits a long weekend.

Beach plus a second chapter

Pair a resort stay with a safari or a few city nights so the trip has one slow part and one memorable part.

Seasonality is where I earn my keep. From the New York area the Caribbean is the easy short trip, so I keep my Caribbean all-inclusive notes current by island; a calm week versus a rainy one often comes down to the month and the coast. For long-haul trips I build in the flight time honestly: an overwater bungalow is wasted on night one if you land exhausted. When dates are flexible, I suggest the shoulder weeks on either side of peak, where the weather is close to ideal and the resorts are quieter.

How I read room categories and inclusions before I recommend a resort

Room names are marketing. One brand's junior suite is another's standard room with a sofa, and an ocean view can mean a sliver between two buildings. So I ignore the label and check what changes as the category goes up: where the room sits, whether the view is direct or partial, whether it is in an adults-only or club section, and what the tier adds. At many resorts, specialty dining, premium spirits, transfers, and spa access are tied to the room tier, which is why two couples at the same address can have very different trips.

Before any property reaches your shortlist, I run the same checks. I confirm what is included for your exact dates and room tier, not the brochure version. I look at the layout so a quiet room is actually quiet and not above the entertainment stage. I check for renovations, restaurant reservation rules, and how transfers work. Then I pull a live rate with current promotions, because published prices are usually stale. My couples get a short list they can trust, and if a resort fails I tell you why and offer the alternative.

Frequently asked questions

Which destinations work best for romantic getaways from the New York area?

For a short trip, the Caribbean and Mexico are easiest because of nonstop flights and adults-only all-inclusive choices. With a week, Europe or Hawaii opens up boutique hotels, villas, and city stays. With ten days or more, I start talking about the Maldives, French Polynesia, or a safari paired with a beach.

How do I know if a boutique hotel or villa is a better fit than a big resort?

Ask how you want to spend a day. Everything on site at one price points to a large adults-only all-inclusive. Quiet, personal service, and strong food point to a boutique hotel. Your own pool and schedule for a longer stay points to a villa. I ask these questions first so the shortlist is built around your habits.

Is an overwater bungalow worth the price?

It can be, if the room itself is the point of the trip and you have enough nights to enjoy it after a long journey. It is less worth it if you plan to be out exploring all day. I also check inclusions carefully, because at many overwater resorts meals and transfers are priced separately and change the true cost.

When is the best time of year to go, and how do I avoid hurricane season?

Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November, peaking in late summer and early fall, so couples who want the lowest weather risk in the Caribbean travel December through April or accept some risk for better value. Islands like Aruba sit outside the main belt. For other regions the table above gives typical windows, and I confirm your dates before we book.

What should we book first, the flights or the resort?

Usually the resort, especially for adults-only and overwater properties where the room categories that suit couples sell out first. Once the room is held, I line up flights that arrive at a sensible hour and match the transfer schedule, and I walk you through travel protection before anything is final.
